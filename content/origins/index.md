## Kabyle, a language and a heritage

**Kabyle**, or *taqbaylit*, is an Amazigh (Berber) language spoken in **Kabylia**, in northern Algeria, and among the diaspora. The name also refers to a cultural identity: the Kabyle people are part of North Africa’s Amazigh world. [Discover the Kabyle language on Wikipedia ↗](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabyle_language)

Kabylia is a region of mountains and Mediterranean coastline, with **Tizi Ouzou** and **Béjaïa** as two important landmarks. The Djurdjura mountains and the Soummam valley are part of its landscape. [Read more about Kabylia ↗](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabylia)

Kabyle belongs to the Amazigh branch of the Afroasiatic language family. Its oral tradition now coexists with a body of written work, notably using an adapted Latin alphabet. Tifinagh is also present as a writing system and cultural symbol in the Amazigh world. [Berber at Inalco (in French) ↗](https://www.inalco.fr/langues/kabyle-berbere)

## Massyle, a name with a history

My first name, **Massyle**, refers to the **Massylii**: an ancient Numidian people organised as a confederation and ruled by kings. Their territory lay in eastern Numidia, mainly in what is now eastern Algeria, near the Carthaginian territories of present-day Tunisia. [Read about the Massylii on Wikipedia ↗](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massylii)

Ancient sources distinguish two major Numidian groups: the **Massylii in the east** and the **Masaesyli in the west**. One of the Massylian kings was **Gaia**, the father of **Masinissa**. Initially fighting alongside Carthage, Masinissa later allied with Rome and played a major role in the formation of a unified Numidian kingdom at the end of the Second Punic War. **Cirta**, now Constantine, became one of its main political centres. [Masinissa in the Encyclopédie berbère (in French) ↗](https://journals.openedition.org/encyclopedieberbere/493)
